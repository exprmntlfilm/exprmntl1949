year: 1967
label: EXPRMNTL 4
blurb: >-
  Perhaps the most iconic and memorable edition of the festival, EXPRMNTL 4
  opened the door to a wider variety of forms of artistic experimentation,
  including sound and physical performances, exhibitions and symposia on the
  avant-garde movement as part of its programme. Reflecting the political and
  social tensions of the 1960s, and perhaps as a harbinger of the cultural
  revolution of 1968, the event was marked by protests and interventions led by
  iconic figures of the period, such as Jean Jacques-Lebel taking to the stage
  to announce the ‘Miss Expérimentation 1967’ award, followed by the entrance of
  naked people. Among the prominent attendees were Yoko Ono, Michael Snow and
  Marcel Hanoun, to name but a few.

  In the realm of experimental cinema, Jacques Ledoux wished to organise an exclusive competition comprising films made specifically for the festival. To this end, he established a partnership with the Belgian company Gevaert, which would distribute 16 mm film stock to selected directors and cover the costs of processing. Whilst this ensured that EXPRMNTL had access to a selection of films never before seen, it also provided a major incentive for emerging filmmakers to produce work, as film was expensive and its processing was unaffordable. One of the young directors who benefited from this innovation was Martin Scorsese, whose acclaimed short film "The Big Shave" was shot on Gevaert colour film. Michal Snow’s structural cinema classic "Wavelength" was another film screened at the festival, winning the Gevaert-Agfa Grand Prix.

  In addition to Snow, the jury awarded official prizes to Lutz Mommartz, Robert Nelson, Ake Ärenhill and Stephen Dwoskin. Scorsese’s "The Big Shave" won the Prix de l’Age d’Or.
